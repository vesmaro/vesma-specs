## Context

Before 2026-10-04 the VESMA ecosystem managed its components ad hoc: five
mechanisms for launching services, `pkill` patterns in `ExecStop`, configs
under legacy names, venv overrides. Every component got its own wrapper,
every wave its own mechanism. Three owner directives of 2026-10-04 fix the
opposite principle:

1. **CLI-first** — a single CLI `vesma`; systemd units are generated by the
   CLI, `ExecStart` is one line without `sh -c`, `ExecStop` without `pkill`.
2. **Supervisor architecture** — one supervisor process, the core against
   optional components, degradation instead of a crash, a control unix
   socket.
3. **A single contract interface** — the CLI is not extended per component:
   a component = manifest + conformance.

For this to work, the rules for admitting components must be declarative and
common. This repository is the home of such rules, and the founding ArchCom
had to decide how it is organized: directories, version discipline, who
merges, how conformance is executed, which languages and which secrets
policy.

The specs layer is separated from canon (boundary rule: "HOW they connect →
specs; WHAT was promised before measurement → canon"); the repository is
public under Apache-2.0 and serves as a showcase for external integrators.
That adds two constraints: the secrets discipline is mandatory, and the
integrator-facing part of the documentation must read without knowledge of
the ecosystem's internal context.

The agenda and the inviolable inputs of the session are in the
[founding ArchCom brief](../docs/brief-2026-10-04-archcom-founding.md);
the verdicts below are fixed by its outcome and are not replayed without
the owner.

## Decision

### 1. Structure: spec-per-contract

```
specs/<name>/vN/
  spec.md           # normative spec (Russian, requirements per RFC 2119)
  spec.en.md        # informative EN mirror of the spec (single-commit sync)
  README.md         # EN quickstart for the integrator
  schema/           # machine schemas (JSON-Schema and others)
  examples/         # minimum examples (mandatory for merge)
  conformance/      # the contract's conformance suite
  CHANGELOG.md      # the contract's SemVer history
adrs/               # ADR-NNNN — verdicts and decisions of the layer (at the root)
tools/conformance/  # conformance runner
tools/ci/           # three tools: schema lint, breaking-change detector, bilingual check
templates/          # templates: spec, component manifest, conformance suite
GLOSSARY.md         # glossary: EN term — RU definition
ECOSYSTEM.md        # ecosystem component map
```

Immutability rules:

- a version directory `vN/` is immutable after the version stabilizes:
  text edits that change meaning go through a new version only;
- minor (additive, non-breaking) changes are recorded in the header of
  `spec.md` and in `CHANGELOG.md` without creating a new directory;
- `adrs/` lives at the root: ArchCom verdicts are the layer's primary
  artifact next to the specs, not an appendix in `docs/`.

### 2. Contract discipline: SemVer and the deprecation window

- Every contract gets its own SemVer; `1.0.0` = the first stable one.
- Breaking-change detector in CI: comparison of the new version's schemas
  against the previous one.
- Deprecation window = `max(90 days, 2 minor releases of the contract)`;
  for versions below `1.0` — 14 days.
- During the window both `vN` are dual-supported; removal of the old
  version happens only in a MAJOR. Discipline precedent: the schema 2
  dual transition, vesma-cortex ADR 0003.

### 3. Governance

- Merged by the ArchCom Chair (TL).
- ArchCom quorum ≥ 2, including the zone owner, — for: new contracts,
  MAJOR/breaking changes, deprecation.
- Additive minor fields — TL + committee notification, without convening a
  quorum.
- Prose, examples, tools — TL after review.
- `CODEOWNERS` on `specs/`, `adrs/`, `tools/`.
- An ArchCom verdict = ADR + PR, inseparable: a decision without a recorded
  ADR and a merged PR does not exist.

### 4. Conformance gate

- CI runs the conformance runner on every PR touching `specs/**`.
- Integration of a component into the ecosystem = a green MUST-pass of its
  suite.
- The runner: stdlib + `jsonschema`; it validates declarations only
  (manifests, schemas, suites) and does not execute components. Executing
  components is the supervisor's and its contracts' business, not the
  runner's.

### 5. i18n

- `spec.md` and the ADRs are Russian (the ecosystem canon); terms and
  identifiers are EN.
- A contract's `README.md` is an EN quickstart: how to connect, what to
  implement, what to verify with.
- The rule: "what an integrator does — EN; what the ArchCom decided — RU".

> **Amendment (2026-10-05, the owner's directive on the full bilingualism
> of the public repo).** Every contract carries an informative EN mirror
> `spec.en.md` (single-commit sync; on divergence the Russian version
> prevails); pairing is mechanically gated by CI (bilingual sync check).
> The alternative rejected below concerned a full normative re-authoring
> of the specs in EN — mirrors do not violate it.

### 6. Secrets policy of a public repository

- Never-list: tokens, connection strings, real paths and hostnames of the
  owner's machines, committed `.env` files.
- In examples — placeholders only (`<token>`, `example.org`).
- `gitleaks` in CI.
- A secrets incident = **rotate first**, the post-mortem after.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| A single root CHANGELOG instead of a CHANGELOG per contract | contract releases are independent: a shared journal drifts and stops answering "what changed in this contract" |
| `docs/adr/` instead of the root `adrs/` | demotes the primary artifact: an ADR here is not an appendix to documentation but an equal product of the layer |
| Additive minor fields always through an ArchCom quorum | slows contract evolution. The systems engineer's dissent is recorded: if "additive" fields drift into breaking ones, that is a rollback point — the quorum returns |
| A pytest package as the public form of the conformance runner | a public repo must run on a bare machine without installing a test framework; stdlib + `jsonschema` give that out of the box |
| A full EN translation of the v1 specs | bilingual drift: two normative texts inevitably diverge; there is one norm (RU), EN is the quickstart |
| JSON-RPC 2.0 as the control-socket protocol | the maintenance cost outweighs the benefit for local management; see the control-socket spec (v1) verdict, mentioned here for completeness |

## Consequences

**Positive.** The structure is predictable for external integrators: a
contract is found by name, a version by directory. The conformance gate
makes the "connect without CLI edits" principle checkable instead of
declarative. Governance separates evolution (additive, light) from breaking
changes (quorum, window, dual support).

**Commitments.** The `vesma` engine and the components consume the
phase-1 specs: `component-manifest`, `service-lifecycle`, `control-socket`,
`layout` (see the [roadmap](../docs/roadmap.md), phase 1). The CI gates
(markdown lint, the breaking detector, the conformance runner, `gitleaks`)
are mandatory before merging contracts — without them the gate does not
exist.

**Rollback points.** Restart-policy numbers are calibrated after the B0
telemetry window: migrating production to the supervisor architecture is
strictly after 2026-10-16. The recorded dissent on additive fields is a
governance rollback point in case of drift.

**v2 horizon** (deliberately outside this ADR): implementing the system
scope of the layout, the group access model for the control socket,
artifact signatures, `sd_notify`, resource limits, `SystemCallFilter`, a
full EN translation of the specs.
